n-test-reviewer — UX Design Validation Report

_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/ux-designs/ux-n-test-reviewer-2026-08-29/{DESIGN.md, EXPERIENCE.md}

DESIGN.md is close to handoff-ready: the token layer is complete and dark-paired, the sections are canonical and in order, and the accent-split / paper-vs-control / flat-bordered rules are stated tightly enough to be enforced in code review. EXPERIENCE.md is not. It is excellent on the four PRD journeys and on every decision the coaching session interrogated directly, and materially incomplete on everything the session never reached: the FR-25 grade dispute, FR-24a parent-side Explanation review, FR-15 timer configuration, FR-33 deletion, and profile management have surfaces named in the IA (or not named at all) with no behaviour, no states, and no components behind them. Separately, the single most concrete accessibility decision in the memlog — the four-carrier grade-state encoding — was dropped in distillation and two of its four literal labels were silently changed.

Three further lenses move the picture, and not in the spines' favour. PRD fidelity finds that two of the six self-declared "deliberate departures" are mis-classified as gap closures when the PRD states the opposite — one of them, FR-15 timer expiry, is a live contradiction producing two incompatible scoring and Mastery rules that no document flags as an override. Coherence finds the Generation Allowance denominated in two different units across the artifacts, with each mock picking a different one, so the mock an implementer copies decides the product's economics. Accessibility finds the flat-with-borders decision produced a system whose only two separation devices both sit below 3:1, making every control boundary in the product an SC 1.4.11 failure, and finds the timed test — hard case 2, the product's core surface — entirely unresolved: no exemption argued, no pre-expiry warning, no timer announcement, work destroyed silently. All three lenses independently found the grade-state encoding broken, which is signal, not noise. The blocking set below is what genuinely stops architecture; everything under it can be absorbed into an Update pass.

Blocking set — do not hand off to architecture until these are closed

Eight items. Each either commits architecture to a stored computation, an economic model, or a conformance claim that cannot be reversed cheaply, or names an entire feature area a story workflow would have to invent.

  1. FR-15 timer expiry: two incompatible scoring rules. FR-15 says unanswered Questions are graded incorrect; the spines say they stay unanswered and are excluded from the Mastery denominator. Filed as a gap closure on the false premise that the PRD is silent, so it does not appear in PRD overrides where it belongs. Architecture reading the PRD and architecture reading the spines produce different scores, different Mastery, and different Weak Area calls on the same Attempt. Blocking — this is a stored-computation fork, not a copy fix.
  2. Generation Allowance is denominated in two incompatible units. key-capture.html counts Practice Tests ("0 of 2 practice tests this month"); key-analytics.html counts requests ("Generations left this month 2 of 2"). Under capture's rule a Free parent can never exceed 2 tests a month, which collides directly with FR-10's up-to-5-per-request and empties the "draft 2 of 5" review-progress requirement. Under analytics' rule the same parent gets 10. Blocking — it is the tier economics and the allowance data model, and a PRD-facing decision against FR-31.
  3. FR-25 has no Parent View destination and no parent grade override. The raising end is buildable and the receiving end does not exist: no IA row, no override control, no score-and-Mastery recompute, no state for what the student sees after an override lands. A score-mutating action is entirely absent from the UX contract, and UJ-2 asserts a destination the IA does not contain. Blocking — an entire feature area with PRD-specified consequences a story workflow cannot invent.
  4. The Mastery formula drops FR-26's rolling window and first-Attempt-only rule. The spine states correct / (correct + incorrect) per Topic as the whole rule. FR-26 computes it over the 5 most recent qualifying Attempts, where only the first Attempt on a Practice Test qualifies. This is the most implementable-looking sentence in the document and the one an engineer will copy. SM-C3 exists specifically to catch the retake loop this omission enables. Blocking — same class as item 1.
  5. The grade-state encoding is broken on all three axes at once. The four decided non-colour carriers were never carried into either spine, so "never colour alone" is an assertion a builder cannot implement; two of four labels drifted from the decided strings, in opposite directions across the two mocks; and the colour assignment is inverted in key-results.html, pulling info into duty as a grade state and putting warning on double duty as both "you ran out of time" and "this is a weak area" over shared components. Found independently by rubric, coherence, and accessibility. Blocking — it is the visual contract for the four-valued state the entire parent-facing value chain reads.
  6. divider at 1.30:1 light / 1.27:1 dark is the sole boundary of every control. A direct SC 1.4.11 failure, created by the flat-with-borders decision: shadows are banned and the paper→default step is 1.06:1, so quiet buttons, page thumbnails, question-map cells and text inputs have nothing else defining their edge. DESIGN.md names exactly two separation devices and both are below 3:1. Rated high by accessibility, not critical — but blocking anyway, because the fix is one token added now versus every bordered component revisited later.
  7. SC 2.2.1 on the timed test: exemption named but never argued, and no configuration surface to argue it from. The design diagnosed the hard case and left it open. There is no pre-expiry warning, no role="timer", no threshold announcement, and two of three timer instances have no aria-label — a blind student's first notice of expiry is that the results screen has replaced their test. The natural "the duration is adjustable" argument cannot be made, because FR-15's parent-side configuration control appears on no screen while the Practice Test card confidently displays "timer presence and duration when set". Blocking — a conformance claim on the product's core surface, and the missing surface is the same one item 1 turns on.
  8. FR-1 sign-up / sign-in / consent and FR-33 deletion have no surface. Of the nine FRs with no UX at all, these two are the blocking subset. Authentication is the first screen every real user sees, carries the PRD's one security-relevant message rule (duplicate-email wording that must not leak account existence), and §5.2 makes the child-data consent notice a launch gate blocking public registration. FR-33 specifies password re-authentication — not the Parent PIN — for profile and account deletion, which is the one place the PIN-only model of Parent View is deliberately insufficient, and it exists nowhere. §4.9's preamble says these were written as FRs precisely so downstream story creation could not drop them. Blocking. The rest of the nine (FR-3 profile management, FR-24a parent-side Explanation review, FR-13 last-Question deletion, FR-31 at-limit block) are not — see below.

Assessed and not blocking

  • FR-32's missing Source Test / Page Image surface — blocking-adjacent. It is a new Parent View destination, so it has IA consequences, but the expired-state rule is one row and the surface can be specified in an Update without changing the architecture's shape.
  • FR-3 profile management, FR-24a parent-side Explanation review, FR-13 last-Question deletion, FR-31 profile at-limit state — real gaps, all closeable as State Pattern and Component Pattern rows against requirements the PRD already states precisely.
  • Mock-level accessibility semantics (zero <button> on the capture and results screens, missing prefers-reduced-motion, tap targets under the stated floor) — these are defects in the reference artifacts, not in the contract. They must be fixed before the mocks are used as an implementation reference, which is not the same gate.
  • Token hygiene, contrast-table inconsistency, copy drift, memlog citation errors — absorb into the Update pass.

thin

FR-15 timer: no configuration surface, and no accessible expiry treatment

EXPERIENCE.md § IA, § Component Patterns, § Accessibility Floor; key-take-test.html
Two distinct aspects of the same absent feature. (a) No configuration surface. The optional per-Practice-Test countdown is load-bearing in DESIGN.md (timer-display, {typography.timer}), in Component Patterns, State Patterns, Accessibility Floor hard case 2, and in UJ-1 step 9 and UJ-2 step 2 — but nothing says where a parent turns it on or sets a duration. The Accessibility Floor asserts the timer is "optional, parent-controlled, and default off" with no control to point at, and the Practice Test card displays "timer presence and duration when set". A feature is displayed that nothing in the spine lets the parent enable. (b) No accessible expiry treatment. No pre-expiry warning is specified anywhere; the timer is a static <span> with no role="timer", no aria-live, no threshold behaviour, and only one of three instances carries an aria-label (States 2 and 3 announce as bare digits). A sighted student paces themselves against a falling number; a screen-reader user's first notice of expiry is that the results screen has replaced their test. Aspect (b) is the only place in the product where an accessibility gap directly destroys student work, and it compounds aspect (a): the SC 2.2.1 "adjust" argument requires a duration the parent can genuinely change, which requires the control that does not exist.
Fix: name the surface that owns timer configuration (Draft review is the natural home — it is the last parent gate before release) with default off, per-Practice-Test, duration entry, and post-release editability stated. Then: role="timer" with aria-live="off" in steady state; polite announcements at 5 minutes, 1 minute and 20 seconds with a matching visible label change carried by text, not motion or colour; role="alert" at expiry fired before the route change, with focus landing on the results heading; restore aria-label on every timer instance.

Recomputed: #DCE3E9 on #FFFFFF = 1.30:1; on #F6F8FA = 1.22:1; #26333F on #16202C = 1.27:1. Required by SC 1.4.11: 3:1. Focus has no token, no width, no offset, and is rendered on one mock of four

DESIGN.md § Elevation & Depth; key-take-test.html only
DESIGN.md commits every interactive surface in the product to "a visible outline in the surface's primary" for focus and "a subtle background tint toward background-default" for hover, with no outline width, no offset, no tint value and no opacity. This is the one place downstream code cannot mirror the spine, and it applies to every control on every screen. The flat-with-borders decision removes shadow as a focus carrier, which makes the focus specification load-bearing rather than incidental. In the artifacts, key-take-test.html carries the only two :focus-visible rules in the set; key-results.html, key-analytics.html and key-capture.html have zero. The strategy itself passes — accessibility recomputed all five ring-on-surface pairs at 5.60:1 to 8.14:1 — but one hazard is uncovered: a primary-coloured ring drawn on a primary-filled button is 1:1 against the fill. key-take-test.html avoids this by accident, via a 2px offset that puts paper between fill and ring. That mechanism needs stating, not inheriting.
Fix: add focus-ring: { width: 2px, offset: 2px, color: '{colors.primary}' } to the frontmatter with the 3:1 non-text check stated, add colors.hover-tint / -dark as hex (or an explicit alpha over background-paper), state that the ring is always evaluated against the surface behind the control and never against a filled ground, and render it on all four key screens.

3. Component coverage

thin

Eight components have both halves and both halves are real rules: Practice Test card, Question container, Explanation panel, Answer-key row, Question map cell, Mastery row, Page thumbnail, Timer display. Naming is byte-identical across all sections of both files. Everything below that line is where the spine stops being a contract. Three lenses independently found the grade-state encoding broken, in three different ways that compound: the carriers were dropped, the labels drifted, and the colours are inverted in the reference mock.

critical

The four non-colour carriers were decided, marked "to be carried into the spines", and never carried

memlog Q66; DESIGN.md § Components → Answer-key row; EXPERIENCE.md § Grade States, § Accessibility Floor #4
The memlog resolves this concretely as the canonical pattern: three redundant non-colour carriers plus hue — (a) icon frame shape and border style (solid circle / solid circle / dashed circle / dotted square), (b) glyph (check / cross / dash / ellipsis), (c) literal text label, (d) row left-rule texture (solid / 45° hatch / dashed / dotted) — with correct and incorrect deliberately sharing a frame shape and separated on every other axis. Neither spine carries any of it. DESIGN.md says "icon + text label + color"; EXPERIENCE.md says "Icon + [label]" with no icon named anywhere in the product. No icon set, glyph, or shape is specified in either file, so "never colour alone" is an assertion a builder cannot implement, and Accessibility Floor hard case 4 rests on it. An implementer reading only the spines builds four differently-coloured circles with four glyphs and believes the requirement is met, while the mock they are asked to match encodes state in border style and row-rule texture the spines never mention. The 45° hatch left-rule for incorrect exists only inside one HTML file. Accessibility separately verified all four states are mutually distinguishable without hue on three independent carriers as rendered — which is the point: the mock over-delivers and the contract under-delivers.
Fix: restore the four-carrier table into DESIGN.md § Components under answer-key row (icon-frame and left-rule specs are visual) and into EXPERIENCE.md § Grade States as the normative encoding.
Found by: rubric (critical) · coherence H2 (high) · accessibility (hard case 4 "partially resolved" + R6)

Both are right about different objects — the decision is deliberate, the mock's self-description of it is false.
