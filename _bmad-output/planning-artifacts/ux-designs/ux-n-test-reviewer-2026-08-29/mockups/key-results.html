A baker has 47 cookies. She packs them into boxes that hold 6 cookies each. How many boxes does she fill, and how many cookies are left over?

You answered8 boxes, 1 left over
Correct answer7 boxes, 5 left over
Hide explanation

Explanation

You said 8 boxes with 1 cookie left over. Here is what happens when 47 cookies go into boxes of 6.

Each full box uses 6 cookies. 6 × 7 = 42, and 6 × 8 = 48. Since 48 is more than 47, there are not enough cookies for 8 full boxes.

So 7 boxes get filled. That uses 42 cookies, and 47 − 42 = 5 cookies stay on the table. Five is fewer than six, so they only fill 56 of another box — not a whole one.

The answer is 7 full boxes with 5 cookies left over. When you divide and something is left over, the leftover part is called the remainder.

Explanation at cap — the Free tier’s 10 new explanations for the month are used up

The only allowance wall in the product that lands on a person who cannot act on it. Noah cannot upgrade, cannot request more, and did not know a limit existed — there is no running counter in Student Mode by design, because a visible “6 of 10 left” would teach a child to ration the one feature the product wants him to use. So the wall must be a plain statement of the plan’s limit and its reset date, and nothing else. Copy rules honoured here: it blames the plan, never the child; it never implies he asked too many questions or did anything wrong; no exclamation marks; no apology; no upsell aimed at a 10-year-old (the parent is the only person who can change the tier, and she sees the counter in Parent View). Everything else stays functional: the full answer key is intact, every previously generated Explanation is still open and readable — reading is never capped, only generating — the grade-dispute flag still works, and retake is unaffected. Put these numbers in order from least to greatest: 0.5, 0.45, 0.55

Correct answer0.45, 0.5, 0.55

Explanation

Line the three numbers up by their decimal points and give them all the same number of places: 0.50, 0.45, 0.55.

Now compare the tenths first. 0.45 has 4 tenths, 0.50 has 5 tenths, 0.55 has 5 tenths. So 0.45 is the smallest, and it comes first.

0.50 and 0.55 both have 5 tenths, so look at the hundredths: 0 against 5. That makes 0.50 smaller than 0.55.

In order from least to greatest: 0.45, 0.5, 0.55.
