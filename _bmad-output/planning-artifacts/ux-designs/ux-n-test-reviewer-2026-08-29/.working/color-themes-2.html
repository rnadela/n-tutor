n-test-reviewer — Clear Room: three ways to make Student Mode and Parent View different rooms

Palette is settled: Clear Room — cool clinical daylight, neutral, zero opinion. Open question on this page only: how the two surfaces diverge inside that family. Crossing the 4-digit Parent PIN should feel like walking into a different room of the same building, not into a different building. Twelve panels: three mechanisms × (Student, Parent) × (light, dark).

01

Temperature shift — same hues, two white points

The divergence lives in the neutral white point. Noah's room is lit warm: paper drifts to a faint cream, the divider warms, and primary softens one step toward a muted mid-blue so nothing snaps. Maria's room is lit cool: paper goes faintly blue-white and primary stays the full clinical #0B5FA5. Semantic hues (correct / incorrect / weak area) are untouched, so a wrong answer reads identically in both rooms — only the air temperature changed.

Held constant from base: success #0F6B4F/#6FD1AC, error #B3261E/#F19B94, warning #8A5A00/#E3B457, info, and all type scale / radii / spacing. Diverged: background.default, background.paper, divider, text.secondary, primary.

02

Surface inversion — Parent View is a back-office, even in light mode

Hues are held nearly constant; the divergence is entirely in elevation and ground. Student Mode is the untouched Clear Room: high-white, airy, generous. Parent View renders on deep slate in both modes — light mode simply means a slightly lifted slate, dark mode a deeper one. Maria's room is dim and dense by construction, so a 30-second kitchen scan lands on data, not on white space. Crossing the PIN is the most physical of the three: the lights change.

Held constant from base: primary hue family, success, error, warning, info, type scale, radii. Student light and Student dark are the Clear Room base verbatim. Diverged: Parent background.default, background.paper, divider, text.primary, text.secondary — and Parent light borrows the dark semantic ramp because its ground is dark.

03

Accent split — identical rooms, different fixtures

Every neutral is byte-identical across the two surfaces: same paper, same default, same divider, same text ramp, same semantics. The only thing that diverges is palette.primary — the color of every interactive element. Student gets a calm desaturated teal that reads as "yours, and nothing is at stake"; Parent keeps clinical #0B5FA5. The room is the same room; someone swapped the light fixtures and the door handles.

Held constant from base: background.default, background.paper, divider, text.primary, text.secondary, success, error, warning, info, type, radii — everything except primary. Diverged: primary only (#0F6E78 light / #71C3CE dark for Student).

One MUI theme; Student Mode wraps its subtree in a ThemeProvider with a single createTheme(base, {palette:{primary:{main:'#0F6E78'}}}) override per mode. Accessibility notes applying to all twelve panels. Every body-text pair listed above is ≥4.5:1 against the surface it actually sits on (both paper and default measured). Correct / incorrect are triple-encoded: a check or cross glyph, the words "Correct" / "Not correct", and color; the left border weight repeats the signal a fourth time. Weak Area is encoded by caret glyph, the words "Weak area · below 60%", the numeric percentage, and color. No streaks, badges, mascots, points, or exclamation marks appear anywhere — a wrong answer states the correct answer plainly and offers "Explain this" as the next action, never a penalty.
