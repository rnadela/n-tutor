- (decision) Q3 resolved: SHAPE SPLIT BY ROLE — paper vs control. Paper role (square, 0-2px): the question container, the test-taking surface, the answer-key/results question rows, the draft-review question list, and the Explanation panel — anything presenting generated Question or Explanation content. Control role (rounded, 8px): Practice Test cards, buttons, inputs, chips, page thumbnails, dialogs, snackbars, tab controls. The rule is semantic and must be stated explicitly in DESIGN.md Shapes: radius encodes IS-THIS-PAPER-OR-A-CONTROL, so a rounded corner means tappable/interactive chrome and a square corner means test content. Named risk to guard at review: without the rule applied consistently this reads as inconsistency rather than meaning; every new component must be classified paper or control before it gets a radius.
- (decision) Q4 resolved: FLAT WITH BORDERS. No drop shadows anywhere in the product; MUI elevation ramp is not used. Separation is carried by (a) 1px divider borders — #DCE3E9 light / #26333F dark — and (b) the background.default vs background.paper step: #F6F8FA/#FFFFFF light, #0E1620/#16202C dark. Rationale: behaves identically in light and dark (shadows barely register on a dark ground, and MUI's lighten-on-elevation workaround would make depth mean different things per mode), and a flat bordered surface matches the paper metaphor established by the square-corner decision in Q3. Consequences: MUI Paper/Card/Dialog/Menu/Snackbar defaults must all be overridden to elevation 0 with an explicit border; focus and hover states must be carried by border color, background tint, or outline rather than shadow; overlay surfaces (dialogs, menus, the PIN entry) need a non-shadow separation strategy — scrim plus border — which must be specified in DESIGN.md Elevation and Depth.
- (decision) Q5 resolved: TWO NAMED DENSITY TOKEN SETS on the MUI 8px base. density.comfortable (Student Mode): row-height 56, card-padding 20, gap 16, section-margin 32, min tap-target 48. density.compact (Parent View): row-height 40, card-padding 12, gap 8, section-margin 20, min tap-target 44. Components read the density token, never a raw spacing number, so the split is enforceable in code review rather than a convention that erodes. Rationale: spacing is now the ONLY remaining differentiator between the two rooms — type is shared (Q2b), elevation is gone (Q4), semantic colors are byte-identical, and the accent is a single hue (Q1) — so the split cannot be left to per-surface judgment. Tap-target minimums are pinned SEPARATELY from the density token and are a floor that compact must never reduce: 48px in Student Mode because a 10-year-old aims worse than an adult, 44px in Parent View. Device tension resolves in favour of this: Noah's tablet has spare room so comfortable is free, and Maria's small phone screen is precisely why she needs more rows visible, so compact-on-phone is correct. Accepted cost: one extra token layer, and every component author must know which room they are building for — same discipline the Q3 paper/control rule already requires.
- (decision) Q6 resolved: MOTION = FUNCTIONAL PLUS PROGRESS. Motion is permitted only where it prevents disorientation or communicates ongoing work; it is never decorative and never expressive of brand character. Permitted: question-to-question transitions in the test surface, expand/collapse of the Explanation panel, page/route transitions, and a deliberate treatment of the asynchronous generation wait (an unknown-duration multi-second job the PRD says a parent must be able to leave and return to — motion there does real work saying 'still running, not frozen'). Explicitly banned: any flourish on the results reveal — no score count-up, no reveal sweep, no celebratory animation — because that is the exact gamified-ed-tech register the PRD's anti-reference forbids, and it would land on a student who may have just scored 6/15. The score appears; it does not perform. Two riders that apply regardless: prefers-reduced-motion must be honoured across the product (part of the WCAG 2.1 AA commitment), and the countdown timer must never animate per tick — it changes value in place, it does not move, which also protects the tabular-numeral no-jitter decision from Q2e.
- (decision) Q7a resolved: INFORMATIVE Practice Test card on Student Home. The card tells the student what they are walking into before they tap. Card carries: Subject, Grade Level, question count, timer presence AND duration when a timer is set, state (not started / completed with score / in progress), and the completed score where one exists. Rationale: a timed test the student did not know was timed is exactly the kind of surprise the product exists to eliminate — the thesis is no surprises on test day, and that has to hold inside the app too. Constraint from DESIGN.md: the card is CONTROL role, so 8px radius, flat with a 1px divider border, no shadow, and it sits on density.comfortable (card-padding 20, min tap-target 48). Copy must stay low-stakes: state is reported factually, never as pressure or encouragement. Sort order for Student Home still open.
- (decision) Q7b resolved: Student Home is a FLAT LIST sorted unstarted-first, then completed, newest first within each band. No subject grouping — it scales down cleanly to the UJ-2 case of two Math cards without adding a header for nothing, and remains acceptable at fifteen cards across three subjects. Work floats to the top, history sinks. Retention of completed Practice Tests on Student Home is a separate open rider (keep all / expire after N days / move behind a Past tests view) and is NOT yet decided.
- (decision) Q7c resolved: Student Home KEEPS EVERY completed Practice Test visible indefinitely. No expiry, no bounded tail, no separate Past tests route in v0. Rationale: simplest and most truthful — nothing is hidden from the student, and every past test stays one tap from a retake. Accepted trade, flagged for post-launch review: the home list grows monotonically (roughly 24 cards by year end on the Plus tier, most completed), so the unstarted-first sort from Q7b is doing all the work of keeping the list usable and MUST NOT be weakened. Revisit condition: if real usage shows students scrolling past a long completed tail to reach unstarted work, introduce a bounded tail (unstarted plus the last 3-5 completed) rather than a date-based expiry, since a fixed ceiling behaves predictably across tiers while a calendar rule has no relationship to how a student studies. This is a visibility decision only — no Practice Test or Attempt is ever deleted, since Mastery is computed from Attempts.
- (decision) Q8a resolved: test navigation is LINEAR PLUS A QUESTION MAP. Persistent 'Question 7 of 15' counter with Back/Next as the primary path, plus an openable map showing every question in the Practice Test with its answered/unanswered state, tap to jump directly. Rationale: (1) it is the only option that makes the submit-with-unanswered warning actionable — a student told 'you have 2 unanswered' must be able to reach them in one tap; (2) paper tests let you flip pages freely, so linear-only is actually LESS faithful to the paper metaphor, not more; (3) it matches how on-screen standardized tests behave, which serves the 'no surprises on test day' thesis. Constraints: the map must show answered vs unanswered without relying on colour alone (WCAG AA floor), map cells are CONTROL role (8px radius) at min 48px tap target under density.comfortable, and opening/closing the map uses the permitted expand/collapse motion from Q6. Noted for future: the map is also the natural home for a flag-for-review affordance — not in v0 scope, but this choice leaves the door open where linear-only would have closed it.
- (decision) Q8b resolved (three parts). (1) SUBMIT CONFIRMS WITH A COUNT: submitting with gaps raises 'You haven't answered N questions' with a path back to them via the Q8a question map; the student can still submit. Blocking submission was rejected — it would trap a student on a question they cannot do, the opposite of the PRD's low-stakes tone. Silent submission was rejected — it punishes a mis-tap. (2) UNANSWERED IS A DISTINCT GRADE STATE, not wrong. Rationale: scoring a skipped question as incorrect would tell the parent the student does not understand a Topic they may simply have run out of time on, which corrupts the Weak Area signal that is the parent-facing value of the product. Cost accepted: a third grade value (correct / incorrect / unanswered) must be carried through Attempt storage, the results answer key, Mastery computation, and the parent drill-down. (3) TIMER EXPIRY AUTO-SUBMITS. This closes a gap the PRD leaves open — it specifies the timer tracks wall-clock across interruption but never states what expiry does. Auto-submit preserves whatever work exists rather than discarding it, and combines with (2) so unanswered questions at expiry are recorded as unanswered rather than wrong. UX-originated decision for the architecture workflow to inherit.
- (assumption) OPEN CONSEQUENCE of the distinct unanswered state: PRD FR-26 defines Mastery as rolling percentage of correctly answered Questions per Topic and FR-27 sets a 5-question floor for Weak Area detection. It is not yet decided whether an unanswered Question counts in the Mastery DENOMINATOR or is excluded entirely. Excluding is consistent with the reason for the distinct state (do not read a time-out as a knowledge gap) but means a student who skips heavily accrues Mastery data slowly. Including it in the denominator reintroduces the exact distortion the distinct state was created to prevent. Needs an explicit answer before Analytics is specified.
- (decision) Q8c resolved: unanswered Questions are EXCLUDED from the Mastery denominator. Mastery = correct / (correct + incorrect) per Topic; unanswered contributes to neither term. This is the only reading consistent with Q8b — if unanswered counted in the denominator the distinct state would be cosmetic and would reintroduce the exact distortion it was created to prevent. Consequence accepted: a student who skips heavily accrues Mastery data more slowly and takes longer to reach the FR-27 five-question Weak Area floor. AND: the SKIPPED COUNT IS SURFACED TO THE PARENT alongside Mastery — a Topic reads as e.g. 'division with remainders 40% - 3 unanswered', never as a bare percentage, wherever unanswered questions exist for that Topic. Rationale: '40%' and '40% with 3 skipped' are different findings — the first says he does not understand remainders, the second says he ran out of time — and the parent-facing promise of the product is a truthful weak-area call, not a tidy number. This affects the Analytics dashboard, the Topic drill-down, and the per-Attempt results summary; it also gives the parent the signal needed to judge whether the optional timer duration was set too short. Requires PRD FR-26/FR-27 to be read with this refinement; UX-originated, for architecture to inherit.
- (decision) Q9a resolved: results screen is SCORE HEADER then the FULL QUESTION LIST IN ORIGINAL ORDER. All questions shown, correct and incorrect alike, each with the student's answer, the correct answer, and its grade state (correct / incorrect / unanswered) encoded as icon plus text label plus colour, never colour alone. Rejected: sorting wrong answers to the top — it optimises review efficiency at the cost of putting a wall of red in front of a student who may have scored 6/15, which violates the PRD's requirement that a wrong answer never read as punishment. Rejected for v0: an intermediate per-Topic summary band between the score and the list — more useful to a parent than to a 10-year-old, and it delays reaching the specific question the student came for. Noted as the natural first addition if results-screen depth is ever revisited. Rationale for original order: it mirrors reviewing a returned paper test, which is the product's governing metaphor. Score header obeys Q6 — the score appears, it does not perform: no count-up, no reveal animation. Question rows are PAPER role per Q3 (square, 0-2px radius), flat with divider borders per Q4.
- (decision) Q9b resolved: Explanation opens as an INLINE EXPAND beneath its question row on the results screen. The row grows in place; the student never loses position in the list and the question stays adjacent to the explanation, which matters because re-reading the question is part of understanding why the answer was wrong. Uses the expand/collapse motion permitted in Q6. Rejected: bottom sheet and full-screen — both cost adjacency or navigation weight for content a student may read in twenty seconds. Consequences to specify in EXPERIENCE.md State Patterns: (1) the first tap is a FOREGROUND WAIT on a model call (PRD NFR: must stay short enough not to break a study session; cached thereafter, so only the first tap waits) and that loading state lives inside the expanded region, using the progress motion permitted by Q6; (2) the expanded region is PAPER role per Q3 (square corners) and flat-bordered per Q4; (3) the FR-24a bad-Explanation flag affordance lives inside the expanded region, as does the FR-23 grade-dispute flag for the row. Explanation body is Literata 19px/lh1.65 per Q2f. Explanation FAILURE state still open.
- (decision) Q9c resolved: Explanation failure = INLINE ERROR WITH A MANUAL RETRY, no automatic retry. The expanded region shows a plain statement that it did not load plus a retry control the student taps. Rejected: silent auto-retry (B) and auto-retry-then-error (C) — both can silently double an already foreground wait that the PRD requires to stay short enough not to break a study session; a visible failure the student can act on in one tap is more honest and more predictable than an invisible one that just takes longer. Copy rule, binding and to be recorded in EXPERIENCE.md Voice and Tone: the failure is NEVER framed as the student's fault and never as a limitation of their understanding. Use 'Couldn't load that explanation.' — not 'unavailable', no error codes, no apology paragraph, no exclamation marks. The rest of the results screen stays fully usable during and after the failure: a failed Explanation must never block reading the answer key, moving to another question, flagging a grade dispute, or retaking the test.
- (override) PRD OVERRIDE, user-directed and explicitly confirmed. PRD 5.3 / FR-31 states 'AI grading (FR-22) and Explanations (FR-24) are never blocked by an allowance' and 'A student must never hit a wall mid-study-session'. This is now PARTIALLY OVERRIDDEN for the Free tier only. New rule: Free tier gets an Explanation Allowance of 10 NEW Explanations per calendar month. Generation of a new Explanation is capped; READING an already-generated Explanation is never capped — previously generated Explanations remain accessible indefinitely, which is free to honour because FR-24a already requires every Explanation to be retained and readable by the parent. AI GRADING remains uncapped at every tier, unchanged from FR-31. Plus / Family / Internal remain uncapped on Explanations. Rationale for capping generation rather than access: identical cost control, but a student never loses access to an explanation they already read, and only ever meets the limit on genuinely new content. Consequences: (1) this changes FR-31 and belongs in a PRD update — architecture must not inherit the contradiction; (2) a new counter, the Explanation Allowance, joins Upload Allowance and Generation Allowance, tracked separately and reset on the same calendar-month boundary; (3) a NEW at-cap state is required in Student Mode, which is the first allowance wall the PRD's design ever places in front of a child rather than the parent - its copy and behaviour must be specified with unusual care in EXPERIENCE.md Voice and Tone and State Patterns, must never read as the student's fault or as punishment, and must leave the full answer key, previously read Explanations, and retake all working; (4) the parent needs visibility of this counter since only the parent can act on it.

The camera stays open across pages — Maria shoots page 1, 2, 3 without leaving the viewfinder, with captured pages accumulating as an ordered thumbnail strip in view. Rejected: page-at-a-time shoot-review-accept, which turns three pages into roughly nine interactions and duplicates quality checking the FR-8 legibility check already does better. Rationale: the governing constraint is UJ-1's five-minute kitchen path, and continuous capture matches the scanner-app pattern parents already know. Reorder, retake, and delete operate on the thumbnail strip after capture, not inline in the viewfinder. Thumbnails are CONTROL role (8px radius) per Q3, flat-bordered per Q4, min 48px targets. PLUS: CAMERA ROLL UPLOAD IS ALLOWED — Maria can select existing photos as well as shoot new ones, mixed freely in the same Source Test. This closes a real gap: the PRD specifies photos-only and describes in-app capture but never rules out the camera roll, leaving a parent who photographed the test at school pickup with no path that evening. Constraints: the 1-10 Page Image ceiling (FR-5) and the FR-8 legibility check apply identically to camera-roll images; ordering is explicit and user-controlled regardless of source; source (shot vs selected) is not surfaced as a distinction anywhere in the UI. UX-originated, for architecture and a PRD update to inherit.
- (decision) Q10b resolved: the FR-8 legibility check runs as a BATCH, once, after capture and before Generate — not per page at capture time. One interruption instead of one per page. User chose this over immediate per-page checking after the timing trade was surfaced. ACCEPTED RISK, recorded because it is real and should be watched against SM-1 (upload-to-release completion, target >=80%): the check's value is highest while the paper is still in the parent's hand and the camera is still open, where a retake costs seconds. Batching means a blurry page can surface after the paper has been put away, making retake expensive and abandonment more likely — and abandonment mid-flow is exactly what SM-1 measures. Mitigations to specify in EXPERIENCE.md rather than revisiting the decision: (1) the batch result must identify WHICH page failed and offer retake of that page alone, never re-capture of the whole Source Test (FR-8 / UJ-1 edge case); (2) the check must run at the moment capture is finished, before any other step, so the gap between putting the paper down and learning it failed is as short as possible; (3) the warning must state plainly that proceeding is allowed, since FR-8 is advisory and overridable; (4) it must be visible that proceeding consumes an Upload Allowance, because FR-31 charges allowance on successful production — a parent on the Free tier with 2 uploads per month can otherwise spend one on a Source Test they were warned about. Revisit condition: if SM-1 comes in below target, per-page capture-time checking is the first thing to try.
- (decision) Q11a resolved: draft review unit is ONE DRAFT PRACTICE TEST AT A TIME, shown as its full question list. The parent reviews a draft completely, then releases or discards it, then moves to the next. Matches UJ-1 exactly. Rejected: a single merged queue of every question across all drafts (loses the sense of which test is being approved), and a draft-summary index screen before drilling in (rejected for v0 as an extra layer). ACCEPTED RISK, flagged: FR-10 permits generating up to 5 Practice Tests in one request at roughly 15 questions each, so this flow can require up to five sequential review passes over ~75 generated questions on a phone, with SM-5 targeting an edit rate of only <=10% — the parent is hunting a handful of bad items in a long list. With one draft this is right; at five it gives no answer to 'how many are left'. Mitigations to specify in EXPERIENCE.md instead of adding the summary layer: (1) persistent progress context while reviewing (e.g. draft 2 of 5) so the parent always knows the remaining work; (2) review progress must survive interruption — a parent reviewing 75 questions in a kitchen will be interrupted, and losing position would be severe; (3) per-question edit and delete (FR-12/FR-13) act in place in the list without navigating away. Revisit condition: if parents routinely generate 3+ drafts per request, add the draft index screen. Question rows here are PAPER role per Q3 and use Literata per Q2f, since this is generated Question content. Release granularity across multiple drafts still open.
- (decision) Q11b resolved: RELEASE IS PER DRAFT. Each draft Practice Test is released or discarded on its own as the parent finishes reviewing it; there is no batch release action. A parent who generated five drafts may release three and discard two, and Noah's cards appear as each is released rather than all at once. Follows directly from the one-draft-at-a-time review unit in Q11a — reviewing a draft and then being unable to act on it would be an odd forced pause. AND: DRAFTS ARE DURABLE. The parent can leave mid-review and return; unreleased drafts persist in draft state indefinitely with review progress and any per-question edits already made preserved. The PRD data model already supports this — draft is an explicit Practice Test state — but nothing in the PRD required the UX to honour it, and this makes it binding. Combined effect: an interruption during review is harmless rather than costly. Whatever was released stays released, whatever was not stays an editable draft, and nothing is lost. This matters directly to SM-1 (upload-to-release completion >=80%), since the review step is the last gate before release and a parent reviewing up to 75 questions in a kitchen will be interrupted. Consequence to specify: draft Practice Tests need a visible home in Parent View so a returning parent can find them — pending drafts must be discoverable, not only reachable by resuming an in-flight flow.
- (decision) Q12a resolved: the Analytics dashboard is scoped to ONE STUDENT PROFILE AT A TIME, with an explicit profile switcher. No combined multi-child view and no all-children overview strip in v0. Rationale: Mastery, trend, and Weak Areas are per-child by definition, Topics are not comparable across Grade Levels, and one child is the Free and Plus reality (tier limits are 1 and 2 profiles).
